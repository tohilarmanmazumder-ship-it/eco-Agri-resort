THE PRINCIPLE

Not just a farm.
A connected ecosystem.

Most farm technologies are adopted in isolation — a pump here, a greenhouse there. EcoAgri Resort instead treats water, crops, livestock, energy and waste as one circulating system, where every resource is used efficiently and, where safely and technically appropriate, recovered and reused.

This is a working model built for exhibition. Figures on this site describing potential resource recovery are conceptual and demonstration-based unless stated otherwise.

SMART WATER MANAGEMENT

From rainfall to reuse

Rainwater harvesting → storage → distribution → hydroponics / drip irrigation → recovery → treatment & quality check → appropriate reuse.

🌧️

Harvesting

Roof and surface run-off is channelled into collection points during rainfall.

🛢️

Storage

Collected water is held in tanks or storage structures sized to expected demand.

🚿

Distribution

Pumps and pipes move stored water to hydroponic channels and drip lines.

♻️

Recovery & reuse

Where technically appropriate and after quality checks, water is recovered for further appropriate use.

Water reuse always depends on appropriate treatment and a quality assessment before reuse — this project does not assume all recovered water is automatically safe to reuse for every purpose.

Rainwater harvesting calculator Estimate

Uses a standard estimation formula. Actual collection depends on roof material, first losses, and rainfall distribution — treat this as a planning estimate, not a guarantee.

HYDROPONICS

Growing plants without soil

Hydroponics is a method of growing plants without soil by supplying water and essential mineral nutrients through a controlled nutrient solution. It is one possible farming method — not a universal replacement for soil farming.

Storage → Pump → Channels → Plants → Collection → Recirculation

A nutrient solution is pumped from the reservoir through channels where plant roots absorb water and nutrients directly, then the remaining solution returns to the reservoir to be recirculated.

Where it may help

Limited land, controlled-environment growing, leafy greens and herbs, water-conscious settings.

Considerations

Needs reliable power, nutrient management, and monitoring — not automatically cheaper or easier than soil farming.

DRIP IRRIGATION

Targeted water delivery to the root zone

Tank → Pump → Main pipe → Distribution pipe → Drip emitters → Root zone.

  • Targeted delivery — water reaches the root zone directly, with less lost to evaporation or run-off.
  • Suitable for many row and orchard crops, though not every crop or soil benefits equally.
  • Automation possible with timers or sensors, at additional cost.
Benefits depend on correct design, emitter spacing and maintenance — a poorly designed drip system may not outperform other methods.

ORGANIC FARM

Soil health, biodiversity and responsible inputs

🌾

Crop production

Crops grown with organic inputs and rotation practices suited to the soil and climate.

🪱

Soil health

Compost, farmyard manure and cover crops build organic matter over time.

🐝

Biodiversity

Diverse plantings and reduced chemical inputs can support beneficial insects and soil life.

Organic farming does not automatically mean higher yield, and it is not input-free — it uses organic inputs such as compost, manure and biological pest management instead of most synthetic ones. Results vary by crop, soil and management.

Connection to livestock

Livestock on the same farm can supply manure as an organic input for crop fields, while crop residues can in turn support animal feed — one of the links the EcoCycle is built around.

BIOGAS SYSTEM

Anaerobic digestion of suitable feedstock

How it works

Suitable organic feedstock is processed through anaerobic digestion (breakdown by microbes in the absence of oxygen) to produce biogas and digestate.

Biogas — a mix of gases including methane, which can be piped for uses such as cooking fuel where the system is designed for it. This project does not assume electricity generation unless a generator is specifically part of the setup.

Digestate — the leftover material after digestion, which can be a nutrient-rich input for fields after appropriate handling.

ABOUT THE PROJECT

EcoAgri Resort

ProjectEcoAgri Resort
ThemeIntegrated Sustainable Agriculture
Prepared byTohil Arman Mazumder

SchoolJawahar Navodaya Vidyalaya, Monacherra, Hailakandi, Assam

Aim

To design and demonstrate an integrated model of sustainable farming that connects water, crop, livestock, energy and waste-recovery systems, alongside a digital tool that helps ordinary farmers apply similar thinking to their own land.

Objectives

Demonstrate rainwater harvesting and reuse; show hydroponics, drip irrigation and organic farming side by side; integrate livestock and biogas; incorporate renewable energy; and provide farmers a simple digital guidance tool.

Innovation

The core innovation is treating these technologies as one connected ecosystem rather than isolated add-ons, and extending that ecosystem digitally through the Farm Mentor, which reasons over a farmer's own location, soil, water, land and goals.

Working principle

Resources move in a loop: water is collected, stored and distributed to production systems; organic outputs from farming and livestock are processed for recovery; recovered resources and renewable energy support the next cycle.

Sustainability

The model is described as a zero-waste-oriented, resource-recovery system — not a claim of zero waste or zero pollution.

Future scope

Physical sensor integration, real measured impact data, expanded crop and regional datasets, and wider farmer testing of the Digital Farm Mentor.
